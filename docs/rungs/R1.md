# Rung R1: fewer atomics

## What changes from R0

R0 adds each absorbed weight to two global arrays with `atomicAdd` at every absorption event: once to the per-batch, per-layer total and once to the depth profile. A photon in the benchmark tissue has about 1,500 events, so that is about 3,000 global atomic adds per photon, and about 10⁹ per second at R0's measured speed.

R1 changes one thing. The absorbed weight per layer is accumulated in the thread (a small per-photon array) and added to the global total **once per photon**: at most `layers + 2` atomic adds instead of about 1,500. The depth and radius profiles are unchanged and still use one atomic add per event; they belong to the next rung.

## Why it is correct

- The physics, the random numbers, and the photon-to-batch mapping are untouched, so event counts and reflectance and transmittance must be identical to R0 (they are, on the host emulation, and the cross-engine check tests it on the GPU).
- The per-photon sum is `float`. One photon's total absorbed weight is at most 1, so this is accurate to about 5×10⁻⁸ relative (measured on the host against the CPU's compensated double), far below the validator's float32 floor of 10⁻⁶. Validation on the GPU checks it again.

## What is measured

Same as R0: validation, agreement with the CPU on identical photons, and throughput. In the same Colab session, **R0 is run before and after R1**, so a change in the GPU's own speed over the session (R0's kernel times rose 5% over five repeats) cannot be mistaken for a gain or a loss. Hardware counters for both rungs come from Nsight Compute (`tissuemc-profile`).

## What would count as a surprise

R1 might change nothing. Atomic adds to a handful of addresses can be cheap if they are combined in the L2 cache, and divergence may dominate regardless. That would still be a result: it says where the time is not going. The prediction in `LOG.md` is for the *size* of the change, and why.
